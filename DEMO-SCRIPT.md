# PauseCheck demo: approximately 2 minutes 30 seconds

## 0:00–0:20 | The situation

Show the empty checker.

“A message says your parcel is on hold. It asks for a small fee and gives you a deadline. Before clicking, you need to decide what deserves your trust. PauseCheck helps you pause, understand the wording, and choose a verification step.”

## 0:20–1:00 | Delivery example

Select **Delivery fee**, then **Explain this message**.

“This is a fictional message. PauseCheck highlights pressure to act quickly, a request for card details, and an unexpected delivery fee. Each concern includes the exact wording, a plain-language explanation, and something practical to do.”

Point to the suggested next step and non-clickable URL.

“The next step is to check the order through the official retailer or courier app. The link is shown as text; PauseCheck hasn't opened or verified it.”

## 1:00–1:30 | Impersonation example

Select **A friend needs help**, then analyze it.

“Here, the message asks for gift cards, applies pressure, and discourages a call. PauseCheck explains these clues and suggests contacting the person through a number you already know. It doesn't claim to identify who sent the message.”

## 1:30–1:55 | No findings does not mean safe

Select **Appointment reminder**, then analyze it.

“This example has no supported warning patterns. Notice the wording: that does not establish whether the message is trustworthy. This is context, not a safe-or-unsafe verdict.”

## 1:55–2:20 | Privacy and implementation

Open **Your privacy**.

"Analysis runs locally in the browser using TypeScript rules. There's no message upload, account, external AI call, or saved history. The app also supports mobile screens and keyboard navigation."

Close the dialog and clear the message.

## 2:20–2:30 | Honest limitation

“The prototype checks common English wording. It can miss scams and cannot verify senders or websites. Its purpose is one practical improvement: giving someone a clearer way to verify a request before acting.”
